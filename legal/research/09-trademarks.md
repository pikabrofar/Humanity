## 2. Legal framework (primary sources)
- **Infringement**: federal registered mark - use "likely to cause confusion, or to cause mistake, or to deceive" - 15 U.S.C. 1114(1): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1114 (verified). Unregistered/common-law marks protected under 15 U.S.C. 1125(a): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125 (not re-fetched; UNVERIFIED this session). **Key point: a senior user without registration can still sue or oppose; prior use beats a later registration (cancellation/opposition under 15 U.S.C. 1052(d)).**
- **du Pont factors** (13): In re E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co., 476 F.2d 1357 (CCPA 1973): https://www.transjurlex.com/ustm/476f2d1357.htm ; USPTO treatment at TMEP 1207.01: https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1207_01.html (older 2017 mirror; current TMEP May 2026 at https://tmep.uspto.gov - section not directly retrieved). Per TMEP, the two key factors are similarity of marks (sight, sound, meaning, commercial impression) and relatedness of goods/services; actual confusion is not required. Courts outside the USPTO (1st Circuit, where a Massachusetts seller would be sued) use a different multi-factor test (Pignons factors) - UNVERIFIED here; ask counsel.
- **Applying to register**: examiner will refuse under Section 2(d) over a prior registration/application; descriptiveness under 2(e)(1) for "OS", "Kit", "Voice".
- **2025-2026 changes**: I did not find a 2025-26 case or statute changing likelihood-of-confusion law. USPTO fee restructuring took effect January 18, 2025 (per-class base fee plus surcharges for free-text goods descriptions) - could not verify figures (fee page URL 404); check https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/fees-payment-information.

**du Pont analysis (qualitative)**
1. *Marks*: OculOS vs OCULUS - differs by one vowel, 3 vs 2 syllable endings ("-LOSS" vs "-LUSS"); both derive from Latin "oculus" (eye) and "OS" in OculOS is visually separated by capital letters. Sound near-identical; OculOS ends in "OS", which could read as "Oculus" phonetically or "Oculus OS". Likely FAVORS confusion on sight/sound. Versus huseyinstif OculOS: identical.
2. *Goods*: Meta's goods are VR hardware/software; OculOS is eye-tracking/gaze-control software. Eye/head tracking is a core VR technology, and Meta's Quest line has eye-tracking features (Quest Pro) - relatedness is plausible, which you flagged. Class 9 and 42 registrations covering "software" in general would be broader than VR.
3. *Channels/buyers*: both downloadable software to consumers; low-cost impulse ($5 min) purchase favors confusion.
4. *Fame (factor 5)*: OCULUS was famous in VR (acquired by Facebook 2014 for ~$2B per widespread reports); fame of a mark broadens protection, and the brand being retired may reduce current commercial strength but not necessarily legal strength. Dilution (15 U.S.C. 1125(c)) is a separate theory for famous marks - ask counsel.
5. *Third-party use (factor 6)*: Hometree and others using OCULUS-formative names in cl. 42 would weaken Meta's scope - cuts both ways.
6. *Intent*: The "OS" blend and eye focus could be read as a deliberate evocation. Document good-faith reasons for the name (eye + OS).
**Mitigation**: (a) Highest-priority conflict is probably the same-named huseyinstif OculOS - it is an MIT repo, so the name conflict is real in search results/GitHub/MCP directories even without registration. (b) Consider renaming the module (e.g., a coined or distinct term) or using as a descriptive sub-feature name ("Gaze" / "Eye Control") rather than a brand. (c) Do NOT file for OculOS in cl. 9/42 before a full search.

## 5. "OS" suffix and Apple marks
- **Apple rules** (https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/guidelinesfor3rdparties.html, fetched 2026-10-01): third parties may not use or register, "in whole or in part," Apple marks "as or as part of a company name, trade name, product name, or service name"; "Do not shorten or abbreviate Apple product names. Do not make up names that contain Apple trademarks." Only "Mac" may be part of a product name, and only when the product is Mac-compatible, **is not a computer/operating system**, "Mac" is combined with another non-generic word, and it is not more prominent. Referential phrases ("for Mac," "compatible with") are allowed if the Apple mark is less prominent than the product name and no endorsement is implied.
- **macOS is an Apple registered mark** (operating system software; the suggested generic term is "operating system software"): https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/trademark/appletmlist.html . Apple also owns OS X, visionOS, iOS, watchOS, tvOS, etc.
- Application to this product:
 - "OculOS" and "ManOS" **do not contain** an Apple mark (no "mac"/"macOS"), so Apple's "no Apple marks in names" rule is not triggered by the letters themselves. However: (1) the "OS" suffix mimics Apple's OS-mark naming family (macOS/iOS/visionOS) - arguably evokes "operating system" and invites a descriptiveness refusal and consumer confusion that these are operating systems; (2) a gazing-control app on a Mac might lead users to think it is an Apple/OS-level feature. Apple's App Store Review Guidelines can reject names implying OS-level status or endorsement - check https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ (not fetched; UNVERIFIED). Distribution is DMG + Gumroad, so App Store review is not a gate unless you later use the Mac App Store.
 - **Descriptive disclaimers**: Use "for Mac" or "for macOS" only in referential phrases ("eye tracking for macOS 14+"), never "Mac" inside the product name unless it meets the Mac-formative rule above (MacHumanity not recommended). Do not use "Humanity OS". Include the standard attribution: "macOS is a trademark of Apple Inc., registered in the U.S. and other countries. This product is not affiliated with or endorsed by Apple Inc." Do not use the Apple logo or Mac logo (separate license: https://developer.apple.com/licensing-trademarks/files/mac_logo_gdlns.pdf).
 - TMEP generic/descriptive: "OS" for software that is not an operating system is likely merely suggestive, but if the software is described as an "OS" the USPTO may require a disclaimer. UNVERIFIED (no TMEP section fetched). The modules are apps/utilities, not operating systems, so avoid describing them as OS in marketing, ID of goods, or the README. Consider renaming "OculOS" in public README from "oculOS" collection to avoid the OS claim.

## 6. Recommended product/doc changes
1. Do not state or imply anything is "cleared"; add a README/website "Trademarks" notice (macOS/Apple, Oculus/Meta, Gumroad, OpenAI, etc. belong to their owners; no affiliation).
2. Stop calling the project "oculOS" in the GitHub repo/organization name if rebranding; the name collides with huseyinstif/oculos.
3. Order a professional full clearance search (federal, state, common-law, App Store/Mac App Store, GitHub, Gumroad, domain, social handles) on the final shortlist **before** printing, selling more widely, or filing. Estimated vendor search cost is not verified here.
4. Order of filing priority if budget-limited: only the umbrella mark (e.g., a coined name) - module names can stay descriptive/unregistered (e.g., "Humanity - Eye Control").
5. Naming alternatives to evaluate (none searched or cleared): descriptive module names ("Gaze", "Hands", "Dictate", "Meetings") under one coined house mark; coined words with no dictionary meaning; avoid "OS" and avoid "Mac"-formatives.
6. Taglines that help: "Hands-free control for macOS" (referential use of macOS is allowed; keep macOS smaller than the product name), but taglines do not cure confusion with an identical mark.
7. If keeping any contested name: monitor via USPTO TSDR alerts; consider a coexistence/consent letter (du Pont factor 10), and pre-write a response plan for a demand letter (rename path, date).
8. Business form: marks belong to the user of the mark; with no LLC, the individual owns it. Assignment to an LLC later should follow a written assignment (counsel).
9. International: later filings (Madrid) depend on the US base mark and local conflicts; OCULUS and HUMANITY marks likely exist abroad (UNVERIFIED).
