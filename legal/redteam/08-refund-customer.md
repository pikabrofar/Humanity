# Red team 08: the unhappy customer (refund demand / small claims)

Persona: a buyer who paid $5-$20, is annoyed, and either wants money back or files in small claims / sends a c. 93A §9 demand letter. Not a lawyer; nothing here says any text is "safe" or "compliant". Reviewed commit 1f611aa against current code. Items marked **[ATTORNEY]** need counsel.

Ranked by (likelihood x impact). Code refs are `LicenseKit/Sources/LicenseKit/License.swift` (LK) and `ActivationWindow.swift` (AW) unless noted.

---

## 1. Intel-Mac buyer: binary is universal, claim and gate are not (L: M, I: H)
- **Scenario.** Gumroad copy says Apple silicon. README "Requirements" (README.md:27-31) says only "macOS 14 Sonoma or later" and the GitHub release notes (.github/workflows/release.yml, "Install" block) never mention Apple silicon. Release CI builds `UNIVERSAL=1` (arm64 + x86_64), so an Intel Mac downloads a DMG that opens, passes Gatekeeper's Open Anyway, accepts the key, burns an activation, then fails or degrades (FluidAudio/CoreML, Apple Intelligence, Neural Engine) with no clear message. Buyer says: "I was sold software that doesn't run on my Mac; the free download let me buy before I could tell."
- **Mitigation.** Gumroad draft line only. No runtime guard: grep for `arch(arm64)` / sysctl / `LSRequiresNativeExecution` finds nothing in the repo. `LSMinimumSystemVersion` 14.0 only.
- **Remaining risk.** Pre-sale disclosure exists only on Gumroad; the activation window (AW:44-48) is where the Intel buyer first meets the paywall, with no architecture check. Misrepresentation by omission plus 93A §9 (min $25 damages, fees) is the angle. Refund window (#3) is the real backstop but is a placeholder.
- **Minimal fix.** (a) README.md Requirements: add "Apple silicon (M1 or later) Mac". (b) release.yml notes: same line. (c) Either build arm64-only (drop `UNIVERSAL: "1"`) or add an early check in `License.requireActivation` (AW:8) that, on `#if arch(x86_64)` or sysctl `hw.optional.arm64 == 0`, shows "Humanity needs an Apple silicon Mac" with the refund instructions, before any Gumroad call. Also add a Gumroad "before you buy" first line.

## 2. Refund promise on Gumroad vs. TERMS §9.1 do not match (L: M, I: H) **[ATTORNEY]**
- **Scenario.** Gumroad: "30-day money-back guarantee, no questions asked." TERMS.md:112 says "[14 / 30] days" and "we grant **first-time** refund requests within that window without questions." A second refund request (new device, another buyer in same household, repeat buyer) is arguably outside the Gumroad promise. Terms are "Not yet in effect" (TERMS.md:1-2) with unfilled placeholders, yet the Gumroad page says "By buying, you agree to the Terms of Sale."
- **Mitigation.** TERMS §9.2 defers to Gumroad. LEGAL-COMPLIANCE.md:220-224 already flags 940 CMR 3.13(4) (refund policy must be disclosed pre-sale and honored).
- **Remaining risk.** Inconsistent refund statements are the cleanest 93A "unfair or deceptive" theory (misrepresented policy), and unfilled brackets mean the document a buyer is told they agreed to is not the finished one. Also the clock: refund runs from purchase, not first successful activation, so a buyer who is blocked on install (#4) at day 29 has little margin.
- **Minimal fix.** TERMS.md §9.1: replace "[14 / 30]" with 30 and delete "first-time"; add "Gumroad's checkout shows this same policy." Set Gumroad's custom refund policy to 30 days to match. Add: "If the app does not run on your Mac we will refund after day 30 as well, on request." Ship no sale until all [BRACKETS] in TERMS.md are filled and the "Not yet in effect" banner is removed.

## 3. Gumroad outage or unexpected reply can delete a paying customer's key (L: M, I: H)
- **Scenario.** `failure(in:)` (LK:108-128) calls any decodable reply with `success:false` or no `purchase` a `.rejected`; `recheckIfDue` (LK:132-140) then runs `save(nil)`, deleting the stored license. HTTP status is never checked. A Gumroad error JSON (rate limit, maintenance, "product not found" after a product is edited/re-created, or `productID` mismatch), returned with `success:false`, wipes every customer's key at their next weekly check, and the next launch shows the activation wall (and needs Gumroad up, to re-agree). The comment at LK:105-107 promises outages "never remove a paying customer's key"; that holds for unparseable pages, not for JSON errors.
- **Mitigation.** Network errors and non-JSON pages are retained (LK:117-119, 137-139).
- **Remaining risk.** Mass lock-out event; 93A/refund-demand surge. Hard-coded `productID` (LK:15) means re-creating the Gumroad product bricks all installed keys.
- **Minimal fix.** In `failure(in:)`/`activate`, take the `HTTPURLResponse`; treat only HTTP 404 with `success:false` (Gumroad's "not found") as `.rejected`, anything 5xx/429/other as `.network`. In `recheckIfDue`, require the rejection to be one of: refunded/chargebacked/disputed flags, or invalid key twice on separate days, before `save(nil)`. Add a unit test in `LicenseKitTests.swift`. Never change the Gumroad product ID; document that in RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md.

## 4. Install friction: unnotarized, ad-hoc signed, permissions reset every update (L: H, I: M)
- **Scenario.** 5-step Open Anyway (release.yml notes), Open Anyway is offered "about an hour" after the first block, needs admin password, and Sequoia removed the right-click bypass. Ad-hoc release builds (`PIN_DR=0`, release.yml comment) mean camera, microphone, Accessibility and audio-capture grants are re-requested after every update unless the optional signing secret exists. Customer: "I paid and it took 40 minutes and now it keeps asking for permissions."
- **Mitigation.** Documented in README.md:20-25 and the Gumroad draft; SHA-256 file shipped; Quick Setup in each app.
- **Remaining risk.** High frequency of support tickets and "not as described / difficult" refund requests, plus the (wrong) belief that Gatekeeper means malware.
- **Minimal fix.** Gumroad copy: put "Not notarized by Apple: install takes about 5 minutes" next to the price. README.md: note permissions must be re-granted after updates until signed with a stable identity. Longer term: Apple Developer ID + notarization ($99/yr) is the real fix; until then refund promptly, no argument.

## 5. Lock-out with no way out: offline, firewall, clock, or new Terms version (L: M, I: M)
- **Scenario.** `isUnlocked` (LK:47-52) needs `stored.termsVersion == termsVersion` and age < 60 days and age > -1 day. Any of these puts the user at the modal wall (AW:27: closing the window calls `exit(0)`), and clearing it requires a live Gumroad call with `increment_uses_count=true`. Cases: (a) a Terms bump ships and the buyer is on a plane/offline (new version locks them out until online); (b) a corporate/school network or DNS blocker (Pi-hole, Little Snitch) blocks `api.gumroad.com`, and after day 60 the app is dead and can never re-activate there; (c) wrong system clock (a verifiedAt more than one day in the future locks the app); (d) a Mac left in a drawer more than 60 days; (e) a traveler in a country where Gumroad is blocked.
- **Mitigation.** 60-day grace (LK:21), weekly quiet recheck (LK:19), error text "Couldn't reach Gumroad" (LK:88). TERMS §7 discloses the 60 days.
- **Remaining risk.** The wall has no support email, no "I already paid, lost key" help, no offline route, and the Terms-bump case treats a paying user like an unlicensed one. The user's data (recordings, meetings, calibration) is on disk but unreachable through the UI while locked.
- **Minimal fix.** AW: add a "Need help? [CONTACT EMAIL]" line and a "Show my data in Finder" button that works while locked. License.swift: when only `termsVersion` differs, allow a re-agree that does not require Gumroad (store assent locally, re-verify later), and use `activating:false` for re-agree so uses count is not consumed. TERMS §7: add "if a verification fails for reasons we cause, email us and we will issue a new key or a keyless build."

## 6. "If Gumroad disappears" promise is thin (L: L-M, I: M-H) **[ATTORNEY]**
- **Scenario.** `License.required = true` is a compile-time constant (LK:7); TERMS §10 (last bullet) promises "we will release a version ... that does not require a Key" if sales stop or Gumroad verification stops. But: no deadline; no auto-updater (TERMS §10); no in-app notice channel; old installed builds hard-fail after 60 days offline or at the first invalid reply (see #3); and buyers have no way to learn a keyless build exists. Seller gets hit by a bus, loses the Gumroad account, or Gumroad bans the account (Gumroad ToS §4.2/§7 KYC/discretion per LEGAL-COMPLIANCE.md) and every customer is stranded by day 67. A court could read the promise as a warranty of continued use.
- **Mitigation.** The MIT source lets a user compile themselves (README.md:33-37, TERMS §2.1), but that requires Xcode/Swift, which is not a consumer remedy.
- **Remaining risk.** Promise is unenforceable in practice and arguably a misrepresentation of how long the paid product keeps working. Also the unbuilt "TRADEMARKS.md" referenced in TERMS §15 does not exist.
- **Minimal fix.** TERMS §10: state a deadline ("within 30 days") and name the channel (GitHub Releases page). License.swift: ship a signed-or-pinned fallback, e.g. fetch `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/.../license-status.json` only when Gumroad fails for 7+ days (disclose in PRIVACY.md), whose `required:false` unlocks. Alternatively, pre-commit now to cutting a `required = false` release if Gumroad verification is unavailable for 30 days. Name a backup maintainer. Create TRADEMARKS.md or remove the reference.

## 7. Refund/chargeback revocation edge cases (L: M, I: M)
- **Scenario.** (a) Customer refunded on day 25 stays unlocked up to 7 days plus up to 60 days offline, which the seller sees as a leak and the customer never notices. (b) A buyer whose bank opens a dispute that the seller later wins is revoked while pending (LK:123-124: `disputed && dispute_won != true`), including disputes that are a bank mistake. (c) Partial refunds are not considered at all (`partially_refunded` ignored). (d) A buyer who is revoked mid-use is not stopped until next launch (checks happen only in `requireActivation`, AW:9-10), so a menu-bar app that never quits is never rechecked. (e) After revocation, `save(nil)` deletes the key, so a later "oops, the refund was reversed" needs the user to paste it again.
- **Mitigation.** TERMS §9.3 and Gumroad line "A refunded key stops working"; §14 "Where practical we will give notice."
- **Remaining risk.** Low money exposure, moderate "you disabled my app while my dispute is open" complaints; and actual data is untouched (good: no deletion on revoke).
- **Minimal fix.** TERMS §9.3: add "Your recordings and settings on your Mac are never deleted when a key is revoked." In AW's revoked path, show a link to export/open data folder. Treat pending-dispute as "grace" (keep unlocked until `chargebacked`), or state the pending-dispute rule explicitly in §9.3/§14.

## 8. Massachusetts warranty disclaimer and liability cap (L: M, I: M-H) **[ATTORNEY]**
- **Scenario.** Buyer claims the app is unfit (wrong clicks, bad transcription, speaker mislabels) and cites M.G.L. c. 106 §2-316A (language purporting to exclude implied warranties of merchantability/fitness for consumer goods "unenforceable") and c. 93A §9 (demand letter, 30 days, double/treble for willful, attorney's fees). Small claims limit is modest (verify current cap with counsel); cost to the seller of even a defended claim exceeds the $5-$20 price.
- **Mitigation.** TERMS §11 and §12 contain MA savings language; §12 carves out 93A, gross negligence, fraud; §17-18 keep consumer venue and small claims, "talk to us first" with 30 days (aligned with the 93A demand-letter period); OPEN question 1 asks counsel about "goods" status (TERMS.md:176).
- **Remaining risk.** (a) §11's savings clause says that where implied warranties cannot be excluded they are "limited ... to the remedy in §12", but a cap that purports to limit the remedy for those same warranties may itself be attacked under §2-316A. (b) §12's cap is "the greater of the amount you paid or US$[50]" (placeholder) - when paid $5 the cap is $50 or a pittance relative to any real loss (e.g., a mis-click that deletes files, TERMS §6). (c) §11 disclaims "accuracy of transcription, gaze, gesture or speaker identification" and §5.5 says do not use "where an error could cause ... financial loss or legal consequences", while README says OculOS "typically lands within a few degrees" (README.md "Limitations") - an accuracy claim; keep it, with its basis. (d) §13 business indemnity is a consumer-unfriendly term to include if any buyer is a "business" using a $5 key. (e) Terms are presented after payment (activation checkbox AW:56-60), which may weaken assent for those terms; Gumroad copy before payment is the stronger notice.
- **Minimal fix.** TERMS.md §11: drop "limited to the shortest period ... and to the remedy in §12" for MA consumers and replace with: "For Massachusetts consumers, nothing in §§11-12 reduces your implied warranties or your remedies to the extent M.G.L. c. 106 §2-316A or c. 93A forbids." Fill $[50] with a real amount or remove the floor and say "refund of the price paid plus any statutory damages that cannot be excluded." Have the Gumroad page link directly to TERMS.md, and in-app text say "you can refund if you decline these." Counsel to answer open question 1.

## 9. Accuracy disappointment (L: H, I: L-M)
- **Scenario.** Buyer expects Tobii-grade eye tracking from a webcam, perfect dictation, or reliable speaker labels; macOS 14 gets older on-device speech (SFSpeechRecognizer) and Apple Intelligence summaries need eligible Macs, while macOS 26 gets SpeechAnalyzer (legal/research/13-third-party-terms.md). Result: "not as advertised."
- **Mitigation.** README "Limitations and safe use" (approximate, a few degrees, not a medical device); TERMS §6, §11; OculOS shows measured accuracy from the user's calibration; 06-ftc-claims.md warns against absolute claims.
- **Remaining risk.** Gumroad page copy is the actual sales claim; any "accurate", "AI-powered", "recognizes speakers automatically" wording is what a 93A claim quotes (06-ftc-claims.md rows 21-22). Eye-tracking claims are especially risky for accessibility buyers who may rely on it.
- **Minimal fix.** Gumroad: add one line "Accuracy varies with lighting, camera, glasses and accent; try it in the 30-day refund window." Do not market it to people with disabilities as an assistive replacement.

## 10. Data loss from automatic deletion and unclear data ownership (L: M, I: M)
- **Scenario.** Murmur deletes recordings older than the retention setting when the app opens (`AppModel.swift:118`; UI MurmurModule.swift:140-146; default is `0` = Never since `UserDefaults.integer` default). Voice profiles auto-delete after 12 months unused (`VoiceProfileStore.swift:29`, PRIVACY.md:62). "Delete All Humanity Data" erases meetings, recordings, voice profiles with one confirmation dialog (HumanityApp.swift:599-611) but does not delete `Humanity/license.json` (good, it is not touched). A buyer who picked "30 days", forgot, then needed a meeting from day 45 says "your app destroyed my recordings"; meetings may contain business-critical content.
- **Mitigation.** Retention is off by default; text beside the picker says "Old recordings are deleted when Murmur opens"; confirmation dialogs and PRIVACY.md describe it; TERMS §6 says "Keep backups"; §12 disclaims lost data.
- **Remaining risk.** Disclaimer of lost data collides with consumer-law limits; automatic deletion is a feature the buyer chose, but there is no pre-deletion notice and no trash. Locked-out users (#5) cannot reach their data through the app at all.
- **Minimal fix.** Add a one-time "N recordings will be deleted on next launch" notice, or move expired items to a 7-day recoverable folder. Put "Show data folder in Finder" in the locked window (see #5). Bold "Keep backups; auto-delete is permanent" in Gumroad copy.

## 11. Uses-count and re-activation friction (L: M, I: L)
- **Scenario.** Each activation sends `increment_uses_count=true` (LK:82). If the seller later enables Gumroad's per-key use limit, a reinstall, a second Mac (TERMS §2.3 says "[one person / up to N Macs]", still a placeholder), or a terms-bump re-agree consumes uses, and the key then fails on the user's own legitimate machine. The activation window message for "key invalid" (LK:121) does not distinguish "limit reached" from "wrong key".
- **Mitigation.** No use limit is enforced by the app today.
- **Remaining risk.** Ambiguity over how many Macs one key covers is itself a dispute generator (the Terms say a placeholder). The 8-8-8-8 hex regex (LK:59) rejects any key in another format (e.g., a gifted or bundle key).
- **Minimal fix.** Decide the Mac count and say it in TERMS §2.3 and on Gumroad; do not enable use limits. Re-agree should use `activating:false` (see #5). Show Gumroad's returned `message` for non-valid keys in a debug-safe way ("limit reached" vs "not found").

## 12. Consumer-rights paperwork gaps that surface in a refund fight (L: L-M, I: M) **[ATTORNEY]**
- **Scenario.** EU/UK buyer (Gumroad is merchant of record) demands a statutory refund after activation. TERMS §9.4 relies on a waiver "by activating your Key," but CRD Art. 16(m) needs prior express consent and acknowledgement at checkout, before performance, and a confirmation; the in-app checkbox (AW:56) is not that. Terms also leave dispute venue, seller identity and address as placeholders ([SELLER LEGAL NAME], [ADDRESS]), so a buyer cannot tell who to sue or serve; the §18.3 line "[Option B in the reviewer note.]" is a visible leftover.
- **Mitigation.** LEGAL-COMPLIANCE.md:433-436 flags the withdrawal consent and says to confirm Gumroad captures it. TERMS open question 2 on EU duties.
- **Remaining risk.** Waiver may be ineffective, so §9.4 over-promises; a single Gumroad-only 30-day window is better for the customer than the law in the EU anyway, so honor refunds on request.
- **Minimal fix.** Delete the withdrawal-waiver sentence in TERMS §9.4 unless Gumroad confirms in writing it captures Art. 16(m) consent; fill seller name, address, county, contact email; remove the "[Option B ...]" line. Consider forming an LLC and cyber/E&O cover before scale (TERMS open question 4).
